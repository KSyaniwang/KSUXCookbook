UX Research

Objective

Build a comprehensive understanding of business goals, user needs, and contextual factors. Deeply empathize with users—both internal stakeholders and external end users—to uncover opportunities and constraints.

Research lenses

01

Quantitative & Qualitative

Combine behavioural data with descriptive evidence to understand both what users do and why.

02

Behavioural & Attitudinal

Compare observed behaviour with what users think, feel, and say.

03

Exploratory & Evaluative

Discover unmet needs, then test whether proposed solutions work.

Research metadata

  • Researcher and contributors

Product facts

  • Product, relevant feature, and project
  • Impacted personas and user journeys
  • Links to relevant past research
  • Decision status: research, opportunity, design, collaboration, user stories, or implementation
  • Owner responsible for monitoring the outcome

Research analysis

  • Size and impact of the problem
  • Sentiment: negative, neutral, or positive
  • Relevant company value, KPI, and business impact
  • Observed facts, key insights, and opportunities

Reader targeting

  • Interested parties, target audience, and readers
  • Required level of detail, from fine-grained to high-level

Design brief

01

Project Overview

This gives someone enough context to understand the project without attending every previous conversation.

It should explain

  • What: a two- or three-sentence summary of the experience being changed.
  • Why: the trigger, such as complaints, a launch, or weak conversion.
  • Where: the affected platform—Web, Mobile Web, iOS, Email, or another channel.
02

Business Goals & OKRs

A strong brief separates the business outcome from the task the user is trying to complete.

Make three things explicit

  • Business Goal: the metric the company wants to move.
  • User Goal: what the customer needs to achieve.
  • Success: the KPIs and tracking events that will prove the change worked.
Example

Increase Add-to-Cart by 15% while helping users identify the right product with less uncertainty.

03

UX Research

Show which decisions are supported by evidence and which assumptions still need validation.

Clarify

  • What existing interviews, analytics, audits, or complaints already tell us.
  • The hypothesis the team intends to test.
  • Whether Discovery Research is needed before designing—and what it must uncover.
Example hypothesis

If ingredients are grouped by benefit, users will feel more confident choosing a product.

04

Target Audience

“Everyone” is not a useful audience. Name the specific group whose behaviour should change.

Describe

  • The segment: new users, returning VIPs, acne-prone customers, or another defined group.
  • Their current mindset, context, and problem.
Example

New skincare customers who feel overwhelmed by choice and do not know which product suits their needs.

05

Scope & Deliverables

Scope makes the expected outcome concrete and prevents adjacent requests from quietly expanding the project.

Confirm

  • Required outputs: low-fi wireframes, a high-fi Figma prototype, or dev-ready specifications.
  • What is explicitly out of scope.
  • Whether usability testing is needed and what must be tested.
Out-of-scope example

The sprint improves the product page; it does not redesign the header navigation.

06

Content & Assets

Design cannot be final when the inputs are unknown. Link the source of truth for every required asset.

Include

  • The approved copy document.
  • Creative assets and product visuals.
  • The relevant design-system library and brand guidance.
07

Technical Constraints & Dependencies

Constraints are design inputs. Recording them early prevents a solution that cannot be built.

Surface

  • API, platform, Shopify, Salesforce, or performance limitations.
  • Feasibility decisions required from development agencies.
  • Approvals, data, or upstream work the project is waiting on.

Design Handoff

Delivery package

01

Design context

Problem statement, business logic, user needs, success measures, assumptions, and the reason behind key decisions.

02

User flows

Task paths, linked screens, entry and exit points, alternate routes, empty states, errors, and edge cases.

03

Screen specifications

Layouts, spacing, responsive breakpoints, interaction states, copy, validation rules, accessibility, and expected behaviour.

04

Assets & motion

Production-ready images, icons, animation files, motion guidance, and clear naming for every exported asset.

Design system + JSON

The Figma component library is the visual source of truth. The matching JSON file carries reusable tokens into development so colour, typography, spacing, radius, and component decisions stay consistent in code.

  • Component library: components, variants, states, patterns, and usage guidance.
  • Design tokens: shared names and values for foundations used by both design and code.
  • JSON export: a versioned, machine-readable handoff for web and app implementation.
{
  "color": { "brand": "…" },
  "typography": { "body": "…" },
  "spacing": { "md": "…" },
  "radius": { "button": "…" }
}

Design QA review

Visual consistency

  • Colours, fonts, spacing, alignment, and layout match the approved design.

Typography

  • Correct styles, line spacing, alignment, wrapping, and truncation are used.

Colour & contrast

  • Tokens are used consistently and contrast meets accessibility requirements.

Images & icons

  • Assets are sharp, correctly scaled, consistent, and exported in the right format.

Interactions & animations

  • Hover, focus, active, loading, transition, drag, and motion behaviours work as documented.

Responsiveness

  • Layouts resize and reposition correctly across supported screens, browsers, and devices.

Functionality

  • Navigation, form validation, errors, and micro-interactions behave as expected.

Accessibility

  • Alt text, labels, captions, zoom, keyboard navigation, focus order, and screen readers are supported.

Content

  • Final copy replaces placeholders and terminology is accurate and consistent.
